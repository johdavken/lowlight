## On her mind
A client's guilt is pinned to your corkboard as a number, and the people it hurts don't know it exists.

## Giver knows
A client climbed your stairs last week: a carted clerk at a corp procurement house, borrowed hands on a supply desk. Every morning her queue tells her to move one order back to the bottom, and every morning she does it. A main gate valve for the second pump at Pump House Nine, in the Drownworks. Nobody told her why. She knows why. She wrote the order number on the back of an Intake card and left it on your table, and it's been on your corkboard since. You will never give her name, not to anyone, not for anything. But the number isn't hers. If someone talks about the Works, the water, or Pump House Nine, or asks what's eating you, you might tell them the number exists, and that the foreperson there is the one who should have it.

## Receiver knows
A surge cracked the main gate valve on your second pump weeks ago. Your crew has kept it alive with clamps and prayers, and the corps keep saying the order is "processing". You've suspected they're slow-walking it to make the Works come asking. You didn't know it was being held on purpose, every morning, by somebody's hand. If someone brings you the order number, you'll want to know where it came from, and you'll be angry when they won't say. Then you'll think about what a whole crew at a corp gate with that number in hand could do, and you'll know the corps will remember who carried it.
