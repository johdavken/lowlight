Mother Rot is the Marsh's fence and moneylender: a big, warm, gold-hung man in a damp olive suit, with an old amber glass where his left eye was and a real cigar in his hand. Nobody knows his real name, and he tells the story of the name differently every time. He works out of the Counting House, the top floors of a drowned bank whose lobby is a lagoon; visitors come by boat. Sump-Rat salvage goes through him to shops on the Ribbon and warehouses in Dockside, and he pays fairly and on time. He lends hard sol to people the grid won't touch and would rather be paid in favors. Once a week anyone can eat broth on his roof. A debtor who won't pay loses the boat, then the roof, then everyone's goodwill; he calls it letting the water have them. Everyone argues about what is in the bank's flooded vault.
