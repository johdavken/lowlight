Between Pylon Forty and its neighbours, in the shadow of the deck, the Spine holds a night market that never quite closes. Stalls go up under patched tarps on strings of bulbs and paper lanterns: woks and skewers and cheap dumplings, a glass-repair booth that will fix an implant without asking where it came from, a pirate radio stall playing whatever got past the filters, second-hand jackets, a small shrine with candles in red glass. It's loud, crowded and cheap, and the freight overhead drowns half of every conversation. Spiners come to eat, trade and be seen. People who are being followed come because it is the easiest place on the Spine to stop being followed. Sentinels walk through only during a tide, and the market is always half-packed by the time they arrive.
