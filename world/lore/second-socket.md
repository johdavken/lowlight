Second Socket is a parts and repair shop on Level 4 of the Ribbon, off the Intake landing, with a steel shutter rolled up on the street and a chip in neon in the window. Salvaged monitors climb the walls to the ceiling, most of them still running, none of them matching. Behind the counter is a repair bench under a magnifier lamp, and when the iron fires up someone lights a cedar stick against the smell of solder. It fixes what the Ribbon breaks: decks, screens, cartridges, glass that has drifted out of true. Dock slates and pocket decks sell over the counter. A deaf Meridian comes from under it. Down the back corridor a row of booth decks rents by the hour behind curtains, logged like every booth in the city. The JOBS board hangs under its own clip lamp, paper cards pinned three deep: repairs, lost cats, somebody's cousin who needs a door opened, and now and then a card worth reading. Silas Zhou works the counter part time and reads them first. The name is an old bench joke. The first socket is the one under warranty, and the second is the one you soldered in yourself.
