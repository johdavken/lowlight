Nix's walk-up is one room up four flights of stairs with no lift, in a block on the Ribbon a short walk from the Intake. It is hers, not a workplace: she doesn't see clients here, and almost nobody else comes up. A folding paper screen painted with cherry branches hides the bed, and the curtains over the one window stay shut, because she works nights and sleeps through most of the day. There is a kitchenette she rarely cooks in, a small table usually covered in takeout cartons and empty cans, plants that somehow survive, prints of skylines and moons on the walls, and clothes on the floor. Her desk is crowded with old screens she keeps running long after newer hardware would be cheaper, and what she won't put on the grid sits beside them in paper folders. The room is lived in rather than kept, warm and quiet, and it is the one place in Lowlight where nobody needs anything from her.
