Nearly everything Lowlight eats began as Soma paste, sold by the tub, pressed into noodles, patties and things shaped like fish. Flavor packs do the rest, and people are as loyal to a pack as to a band. Cooking is the art of hiding it. Every family that came over the water brought a way of doing that, and a stall cook is judged by what they can make paste forget. Real food is a different economy. The Long Haul's eggs are real and the drivers know what that's worth. Marsh people eat what they catch from their windows, and nobody upstack will touch it. Anything grown in dirt is a gift, a bribe or an apology, and a whole orange has ended friendships. People lower their voices to say real. Offer someone a bowl and they should take it, because in Lowlight food is how most people say the things they won't say.
