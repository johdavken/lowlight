The Ribbon began as a nickname for a road that twisted through Karasu. Businesses lined it, then built over it, then built over that, until the road became a stack. Each level is a full street of stalls, bars, clinics and rooms, carried on girders over the one below and joined by stairs and lifts. Newcomers stop and stare; it should not stand up. Levels are counted from the bottom. The higher you go, the cleaner, quieter and more expensive it gets, but this is still Karasu, and the top of the Ribbon is classy only by Karasu standards. The lowest levels barely see daylight. The stack winds down out of Old Town and levels out into the Strand.
