Lowlight has no great church. It has landings. Small shrines turn up on stairs, at the feet of pylons and at the backs of markets: a shelf, a saint or a photograph, and candles in red glass. Nobody runs them and nobody agrees who they belong to. Every boat through Tidal Isle brought its own gods, the old Karasu families had theirs, and after enough years they all share the same shelf. People light a candle for the missing, the drowned, the bounced and anyone in the Q. A red glass burning on a landing means someone in the building hasn't come home yet, and the neighbors know not to ask. Even the Breakers leave the candles alone. The corps have no opinion, which is the nearest thing to tolerance Lowlight offers. The Drowned Choir says every candle is lit to the wrong water.
