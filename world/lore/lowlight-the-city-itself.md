Lowlight behaves like someone who has been cheated too many times. It is patient, watchful and never generous. Everything in it is for rent: light, water, air on the upper levels, a clean record, a few hours of being left alone. Darkness is the only free thing, and most people live in it. Neon covers the whole city now, but it belongs to whoever pays for it, and it only shows you what someone wants to sell. The city remembers debts and forgets people. The corps own the streets and the grid. Runners move through the grid unseen. Ordinary people keep their heads down and their business on paper. Public violence is rare, and it is common everywhere else. Nobody calls Lowlight beautiful out loud, but people who leave talk about it for the rest of their lives. The tech is old and patched: humming screens, heavy keys, cables, cartridges, things repaired instead of replaced. The only new thing in Lowlight is the money.
