The Long Haul is a hauler stop built into a Spine pylon at deck level, a little north of Pylon Forty, reached by a service ramp that drivers use and nobody else is supposed to. It has been open all night for as long as anyone on the Spine remembers. A long counter with chrome stools, a flat-top griddle, two coffee urns that are never empty, a pie case nobody trusts, an old jukebox, and a row of oxblood booths along wide windows onto the deck, where the heavy haulers park with their marker lights on and the traffic goes by close enough to feel. A pink neon cup glows in the window. The drivers who eat there come off twelve-hour shifts and don't care who's in the next booth, which is why people who need somewhere ordinary to sit with someone go there. The coffee is bad, the eggs are real, and the corner booth at the far end is the one couples and deals both end up in.
